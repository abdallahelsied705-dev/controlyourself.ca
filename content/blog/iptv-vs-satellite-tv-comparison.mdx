More Canadian homes are letting their satellite subscriptions lapse and moving to IPTV, usually to shrink the monthly bill and get out of a long contract. But satellite still has genuine strengths — especially if your broadband is patchy. Here's how the two stack up on cost, channels, picture, reliability, setup and flexibility, so you can judge which fits your home.

## The two side by side

| | IPTV | Satellite TV |
|---|------|--------------|
| Monthly cost | From about C$7 (yearly plan) | C$65–C$120 with sport and films |
| Contract | None | Usually 18–24 months |
| Installation | An app — about 5 minutes | Dish, box and often an engineer |
| Live channels | Thousands, Canadian and international | 200–500 depending on package |
| On-demand | Large film and series library | Limited, varies by package |
| Picture quality | HD, FHD and 4K (broadband-dependent) | HD and 4K on selected channels |
| Works without broadband | No | Yes |
| Devices | TV, phone, tablet, PC | Mostly the TV with the box |
| Works abroad | Yes | No |

## Cost

This is where the gulf is biggest. A satellite package with sport and films usually lands at **C$65–C$120 a month**, plus any install or box fees, locked in for 18–24 months — roughly **C$1,500–C$2,900 over two years**.

IPTV is a one-off subscription. [IPTV Canada](/) is C$13.99 for a month, C$94.99 for a year (about C$7.92 a month) or C$159 for two years — somewhere around **C$159–C$360 over two years** depending on the plan. There's no box to rent; you use a TV, Firestick or phone you already own.

## Channels and content

Satellite is sold in tiers: a base bundle, then extra for sport, films or kids' channels. IPTV services typically fold the lot into one subscription — Canadian entertainment, sport, films, news, kids', music and international channels — plus a big on-demand library and 7-day catch-up. If you watch channels from other countries as well as Canadian TV, IPTV is far more flexible, since international channels normally come at no extra cost.

## Picture quality

Both can look superb. Satellite gives a steady signal regardless of your internet. IPTV quality rides on your broadband: **10 Mbps** covers HD and **25 Mbps** covers 4K. On a typical Canadian fibre line IPTV looks every bit as good as satellite, and most services offer each channel in several qualities so you can drop down when the connection's busy.

## Reliability

Satellite can be knocked about by heavy rain, snow or a tree in the way. IPTV depends on your home network and the provider's servers. In practice:

- With **good broadband and a wired connection**, a well-run IPTV service matches satellite for dependability.
- With **slow or flaky broadband** (still common in parts of the countryside), satellite is the safer bet.
- Peak times — weekend sport, weekday evenings — are where weak IPTV providers wobble, so always test during a busy period on a free trial.

## Setup and flexibility

Satellite wants a dish on the wall, a box wired to one TV and often an engineer slot. Move house and you rearrange the install; watching in another room can cost more.

IPTV needs no installation: install an app, enter your login, watch. Use it on any TV, phone or tablet in the house (up to your number of connections), take it abroad, and "cancel" simply by not renewing.

## So which is right for you?

**Go with IPTV if:**
- Your broadband is 25 Mbps or more
- You want to cut the monthly bill and dodge contracts
- You watch on several devices or travel abroad
- You want international channels alongside Canadian TV

**Stay with satellite if:**
- Your broadband is below ~10 Mbps or drops out
- You'd rather have one box that works independently of the internet
- You're mid-contract and leaving early would cost more than you'd save

## Frequently asked questions

**Is IPTV cheaper than satellite?**
Yes. IPTV is typically C$4–C$13.99 a month with no contract, versus C$65–C$120 a month for a satellite package with sport and films.

**Is IPTV picture quality as good as satellite?**
On 25 Mbps or more, yes — IPTV streams in HD and 4K. On slower lines satellite is steadier.

**Can I keep my dish and use IPTV too?**
Yes. IPTV only needs your broadband, so you can run both while you make up your mind.
