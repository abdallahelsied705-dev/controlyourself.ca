Premium sport — football, motorsport, boxing, baseball, hockey and golf — is scattered across several Canadian broadcasters. Chasing all of it through a satellite bundle can add C$40–C$60 a month just for the sport tier, on top of the base package and an 18–24 month contract. The good news: you no longer need a dish to watch live sport. Here are the routes, what they cost, and who each one suits.

## Your options at a glance

| Option | Typical cost | Coverage | Contract | Best for |
|--------|--------------|----------|----------|----------|
| Broadcaster day pass | Around C$15 a day | One broadcaster's events that day | None | The odd big event |
| Broadcaster monthly pass | ~C$30–C$35 a month | One broadcaster's full schedule | None | Fans of one sport or league |
| Single-sport subscription | ~C$25–C$30 a month | One competition or sport | None | Followers of one competition |
| Free-to-air apps | Free | Highlights and selected major tournaments | None | Casual viewers |
| IPTV subscription | From ~C$7.92 a month | Sport channels from many broadcasters in one place | None | Regular sport viewers |
| Satellite sport bundle | C$65–C$120 a month total | Most live sport | 18–24 months | Homes with slow broadband |

## Option 1: Broadcaster streaming passes

Most major sports broadcasters now sell their channels through a streaming app, as either a **day pass** (around C$15) or a **monthly pass** (~C$30–C$35), with no contract and cancel-any-time.

**Upside:** official, simple, high quality. **Downside:** each pass only covers that broadcaster's events. Football, motorsport and boxing rights are split between several companies, so following a full season often means two or three passes at once — which can rival the cost of satellite.

## Option 2: Single-sport subscriptions

Some competitions — European club football, combat sports and wrestling, say — sit behind their own service at around C$25–C$30 a month. Good value if you follow one competition closely, pricey if you follow several.

## Option 3: Free-to-air sport

Free-to-air broadcasters still carry plenty through their apps and channels: highlights shows, selected live races, and the big international football and hockey tournaments. If you mainly watch the marquee events and catch the rest as highlights, this may be all you need — nothing to pay beyond a cable bill where one applies.

## Option 4: An IPTV subscription

IPTV gathers the sport channels into one subscription and one app, usually C$5–C$13.99 a month with no contract. It runs on a Smart TV, Firestick, phone or tablet, and most services include 7-day catch-up so you can rewatch matches you miss.

For the best live-sport experience:

- Go **wired** — Wi-Fi is the number-one cause of match-day buffering.
- Have at least **10 Mbps for HD** and **25 Mbps for 4K**.
- **Test during a busy slot** on a free trial — a Saturday afternoon or a weekday-evening fixture.

## Option 5: Keep satellite (if your broadband is slow)

If your broadband is below about 10 Mbps or drops out, satellite is still the steadiest way to watch live sport, since it doesn't lean on your internet. See our [IPTV vs satellite TV comparison](/blog/iptv-vs-satellite-tv-comparison).

## Which one fits you?

- **A few big events a year:** free-to-air plus the occasional day pass.
- **One league or sport:** that broadcaster's monthly pass or a single-sport subscription.
- **Several sports every week:** an IPTV subscription is usually the cheapest way to get it all in one place.
- **Slow broadband:** stick with satellite.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I watch live sport without a dish?**
Yes. Streaming passes, single-sport subscriptions, free-to-air apps and IPTV all run over broadband.

**What's the cheapest way to watch sport in Canada?**
Free-to-air covers the major tournaments for nothing. For regular viewing across several sports, an IPTV subscription from about C$7.92 a month is usually cheapest.

**What speed do I need?**
About 10 Mbps for HD and 25 Mbps for 4K. A wired connection helps most.
